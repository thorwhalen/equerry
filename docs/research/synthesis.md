# Synthesis: what the build takes from the seven reports

2026-11-09. Reads the phase-1 report and the six deep-research reports of this date and says what equerry should be, where the reports agree, where they pull apart, and the order to build in. References are the reports themselves; each claim below is argued in the report named.

## The one-paragraph answer

equerry is a thin organisation layer, not a runtime and not a new agent framework. Its keystone is one record, the **item**: a request for the principal's attention, produced by any agent, manager or source, carrying its recommendation, the representative's predicted objection, a reversibility class, its state (A2A names) and a dedupe key. Everything else is a producer or a renderer of items: the **brief** is a ranked, capped, verified list of items delivered on a cadence; a **reminder** is an item with a trigger policy; the **desk** is the phone page that lists items needing the principal; the **secretary** is a front-desk agent that reads items and the fleet, answers questions, and turns the principal's words into proposals, never doing the work itself; **voice** is an adapter over the secretary's text turns and the desk's verbs. Machinery stays small: a desk store that is the system of record, an append-only event stream, and one deterministic tick on an always-on machine; no orchestrator agent in the control path, no broker, no durable-execution engine, no database server in v1 ([agent-organisation-architecture](agent-organisation-architecture.md)).

## Where the reports agree (and the design therefore commits)

1. **Exceptions, not workers, on the front page.** The desk leads with a budgeted "Needs you" list, with why-now, the recommendation and the predicted objection on every row; agents and sessions are a drill-down ([agent-ops-visual-interface](agent-ops-visual-interface.md); Paperclip's Decisions page in [prior-art](prior-art-ai-secretaries.md); the phase-1 gate in [principal-model](principal-model-and-review-organisation.md)).
2. **Caps and reasons, or the principal stops reading.** "Needs you" at most 7 items per brief, everything else counted behind a link; each item says why it is there; "not for me" and "you missed" are collected from day one and precision of at least 0.7 gates any expansion ([briefs-and-attention-triage](briefs-and-attention-triage.md)); interrupting pushes under a daily budget with severity mapped to the phone's levels ([agent-ops-visual-interface](agent-ops-visual-interface.md), [intelligent-reminders](intelligent-reminders.md)).
3. **Never block an agent on the principal, and nothing but the principal's own surface approves.** A session raises an item and ends its turn; the decision comes back later as a message. Messages from agents, routine payloads and chat senders are untrusted and cannot consent ([agent-organisation-architecture](agent-organisation-architecture.md), [prior-art](prior-art-ai-secretaries.md)).
4. **Consent by action class, in files the principal can edit.** A table of classes (send to a person, spend, delete, merge, publish, change settings) with allowed / ask first / off, saved as revocable rules; the reversibility class is a rule, never a model's guess; memory and rules live in readable files, never only in a conversation that can be compacted ([prior-art](prior-art-ai-secretaries.md), phase 1).
5. **Scheduled jobs that skip when nothing changed, not a heartbeat over the whole context.** Briefs and reminders are named jobs with a cheap deterministic gate, a small model and a budget ([prior-art](prior-art-ai-secretaries.md), [briefs](briefs-and-attention-triage.md), [reminders](intelligent-reminders.md)).
6. **Reasoning stays in text; voice is an adapter at the edge.** Speech-native agents keep only 30–45% of a text agent's task success; every serious voice framework calls the brain as a text stream behind a seam. Ten interface requirements make voice pluggable: speakable names and one vocabulary, a truncatable transcript, cancel, acknowledgement and deferred replies, proposals with a reversibility tier, briefs as structured documents with heard-state in the core ([voice-as-a-layer](voice-as-a-layer.md)).
7. **Use the field's words.** Item states from A2A (`submitted`, `working`, `input-required`, `completed`, `failed`, `canceled`, `rejected`); responses from Agent Inbox (`accept`, `edit`, `respond`, `ignore`) plus `later`; events shaped as CloudEvents; reminders exportable to VALARM/RFC 9074 and JSCalendar ([agent-organisation-architecture](agent-organisation-architecture.md), [reminders](intelligent-reminders.md)).

## Where they pull apart (and what the build decides)

- **Extend the existing task record, or define a new item?** The architecture report says extend the sibling obligation tracker's `Task` and board store (states, revision, review, risk, route, due, remind_at). The interface report says define `Item`, `Actor`, `VerbInvocation`, `Event`, `DeskSnapshot` once as JSON Schema with thin adapters from the trackers. Both are right at different layers: **equerry owns the schema (the contract every surface renders); the obligation tracker gains the missing fields at its own seam so its tasks are items without an adapter.** Until that lands, a thin adapter maps tasks and session rows to items.
- **Who runs the clock?** Briefs: a launchd or systemd timer at 05:50 with the schedule stored as time zone plus RRULE. Reminders: a stateless tick every 60 s plus event hooks. Architecture: one deterministic tick per machine. **Decision: one tick, on the always-on server, owning both briefs and reminders; the laptop never schedules anything that must fire** (sleeping laptops and daylight-saving changes are the named risks). Claude Code's cloud routines and session-scoped cron are the wrong grain for durable reminders and are used only for "in 20 minutes" inside a live session.
- **Where does the brief's intelligence sit?** The briefs report puts an item layer between the existing overview pipeline's `Material` and `Script` and keeps compose and render unchanged; the voice report wants briefs rendered as one continuous audio file with chapters and a sentence-aligned transcript, with private segments routed to a local voice. **Decision: the brief is an item list first (data), with two renderers, page and spoken script; the existing overview pipeline is the spoken renderer and gets chapters and per-segment privacy class.**
- **Prior art to borrow from.** The reports name Paperclip (ticket checkout, dependencies, budgets with hard stops, Decisions page mechanics: why-now, request-revision, expiry, typed confirmation), Gas Town (a coordinator that never writes code, stall detection, severities), LangChain's Agent Inbox (response types), Trigger.dev's waitpoint token (resume shape), and Claude Code's own one-line summaries. OpenClaw is to be studied for its retreat from always-on heartbeats and its security record, and avoided as a runtime. Several hand-built fronts carry non-commercial licences: ideas, never code.

## The secretary, concretely

The secretary is one agent definition with two clients. Text: a Claude Code session the principal talks to (today the lookout plays this role, with no memory of its own beyond ledgers). Voice: the owner's existing turn-based voice manager, whose brain already satisfies most of the contract (read-only tools, read-then-act taint rule, proposals confirmed on a card, limits per conversation). The organisation layer's job is to give both the same tools: list and get items, invoke verbs (with idempotency keys), read the fleet roster, capture a reminder (the LLM extracts a typed trigger; dateparser, a slot table and rrule check it; one echo line confirms), request a brief, and propose. The secretary never performs: dispatch goes to the lookout as an intent, as it does today.

## Build order (the minimum viable version)

1. **Item schema and desk store** (JSON Schema, Python and Zod mirrors), the verbs endpoint with idempotency keys, the append-only event log, and adapters from the obligation tracker's tasks and the lookout's roster. Log every automated decision.
2. **Brief v1**: window since the last delivered brief; sources sessions and GitHub first (chat and mail through the channel facade next); classify by rules then model; cost-of-delay ranking with an irreversibility floor; "Needs you" ≤ 7; rendered to a phone page and a ~300-word spoken script; feedback taps and the "you missed" box; one job on the server at the cadence the principal sets.
3. **Reminders v1**: "when session X finishes", "in N minutes", "tomorrow"; no time means the next brief; the 60 s tick with an outbox and idempotency keys; delivery as the brief, then a quiet push, with quiet hours and a daily budget.
4. **The desk page**: Desk, Fleet (embedding the existing roster page), Log; a `DeskSnapshot` re-read on a timer.
5. **The voice contract** written into the secretary's interface; the existing voice manager becomes its first adapter.
6. **Managers by concern and the meta-judge** (the phase-1 sub-issues), feeding `assessment` into items.

Dependencies outside equerry: confirmed intents must reach a running lookout (an open issue in the lookout package), the obligation tracker's task record needs due/remind_at/review/risk fields, and a push channel to the phone must be configured through the channel facade.

## Metrics the whole thing is judged by

Needs-you precision (target ≥ 0.7) and the near-miss rate; false auto-approve rate; items shown per day and the override rate on them; reminder correction rate, delivery-to-seen latency and acted-on rate; repeat corrections per month; and the principal's own consistency on repeated items as the ceiling.

## What the reports could not verify

Each report marks its unverified items. The recurring gaps: late-2026 pricing for some TTS vendors (taken from the earlier survey), a few product claims resting on one secondary source (several "your day" feeds' current status), two notification-timing topics the interface report did not reach, and Gas Town's cost reports. None changes the recommendations above.
