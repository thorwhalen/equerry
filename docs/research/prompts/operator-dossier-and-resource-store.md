Research theme: the "about the principal" layer — a store of not-too-sensitive facts and resources about the one person an organisation of agents works for (names and how they are written, pronouns, time zone and working hours, roles and affiliations, public links and handles, bio in several lengths, profile pictures and logos, brand colours, signature blocks, standard answers to recurring forms, preferences already modelled elsewhere), so that the right agent finds the right fact or file when it needs it instead of asking the person again.

Cover: what agents ask their principal for repeatedly (evidence from agent-tooling communities, issue trackers, "memory" features); prior art for personal data stores and profiles readable by software: Solid pods and WebID, schema.org Person and vCard/jCard, FOAF, h-card and microformats, humans.txt, Gravatar and Libravatar for pictures, press kits and "about me" pages as a pattern, Keybase-style identity proofs; agent-memory systems with a user profile notion (mem0, Zep/Graphiti, Letta, Claude memory, ChatGPT memory and custom instructions, OpenClaw's user/soul files, Rewind and Apple's on-device personal context) and what they got right or wrong about profile versus episodic memory; privacy tiers and consent models (public, shareable with named parties, agent-only, never) and how to mark a field's tier; retrieval contracts (which agent may read which tier, in what situation, and how a read is logged); how resources (images, documents, templates) should be addressed so a store can move from a folder to object storage; what should never be stored; how a profile stays current (who writes, review, provenance per field, the newest statement wins). Deliverable: a recommended schema (fields grouped, each with tier, source and date), the retrieval contract, a recommendation on whether the profile belongs in the same store as records of other people or in its own, the standard terminology, and the minimum viable version. Also anything important I did not ask.
