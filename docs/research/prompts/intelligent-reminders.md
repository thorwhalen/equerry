Research theme: reminders with inferred timing and channel — a principal says "remind me of X" with or without a time, and the secretary decides when and how to remind, for a system whose outputs reach the principal on a phone (push, chat, voice, a morning brief).

Cover: the cognitive-science basis (prospective memory, implementation intentions, event-based vs time-based cues); the HCI research on interruption management and opportune moments (Horvitz's attention models, Iqbal and Bailey's breakpoints, Gloria Mark's work, context-aware notification delivery, "defer to a natural break"); how shipped products handle it (Apple Reminders incl. location and "when messaging", Google Assistant/Gemini reminders, Siri, Todoist and Things natural-language dates, Slack /remind, Microsoft To Do, Superhuman snooze, Gmail nudges); natural-language time parsing libraries in Python (dateparser, parsedatetime, duckling, ctparse) with licences; delivery channels and their escalation (silent list → brief → push → voice), do-not-disturb and quiet hours, repeat and snooze semantics, confirmation that a reminder was acted on; and the data model: reminder = intent + trigger policy (time, event, context) + channel policy + escalation, with a log. Deliverable: a recommended model and decision rules for "no time given", a comparison of parsing libraries with a verdict, the evaluation (did it fire at a good moment; was it acted on), and standard terminology. Also anything important I did not ask.
