Research theme: voice (speech-to-text and text-to-speech) as an independent layer over a text-based organisation layer — what must be designed now so that voice can be added or swapped without changing the core.

Cover: architectures that keep the conversational core text-based with voice adapters at the edge (speech pipelines in Pipecat, LiveKit Agents, Vapi, the OpenAI Realtime and Gemini Live APIs, Apple's Speech framework, browser Web Speech), and what each demands of the core (streaming, barge-in, turn detection, latency budgets); STT and TTS options as of late 2026 with licence and cost (Whisper and whisper.cpp, Deepgram, ElevenLabs Scribe, AssemblyAI, Kokoro, Moshi, Parakeet; ElevenLabs, Cartesia, OpenAI TTS, Kokoro, Piper), including local-only options for private content; voice UX for the two directions this system needs: commands and short questions in (push-to-talk vs wake word vs full duplex; confirmation of consequential actions by voice) and briefs out (how to write text that reads well aloud; chaptering; pause/resume; the transcript in sync); phone constraints (iOS WebKit audio, background playback, PWAs); evaluation (word error rate on domain vocabulary, task success). Deliverable: the contract the text core must satisfy for voice to be an adapter (a short list of interface requirements), a comparison table with verdicts, a recommended v1 (turn-based) and the path to full duplex, and standard terminology. Note that the owner already has a turn-based voice front end with ElevenLabs and a local fallback and has deliberately deferred full duplex; do not redo that choice, say what it needs from the organisation layer. Also anything important I did not ask.
