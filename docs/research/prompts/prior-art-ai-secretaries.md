Research theme: prior art — AI secretaries, chiefs of staff and "AI company" operating systems (2024–2026), for the design of an organisation layer over a fleet of coding agents.

Cover: open-source and commercial systems that act as a personal secretary, chief of staff or executive assistant for a person who runs many agents or many projects: personal-assistant runtimes that live on the user's machine and talk over chat channels (e.g. OpenClaw and its forks, Khoj, Leon, Open Interpreter), "AI company" or agent-org products (Paperclip, CrewAI hierarchical, MetaGPT/ChatDev, Relevance AI, Lindy, Dust, Zapier agents), the big vendors' assistant features (ChatGPT Pulse and Tasks, Gemini scheduled actions and daily briefs, Microsoft Copilot agents, Apple Intelligence), and the small hand-built "Jarvis" style front ends for coding agents. For each: what it is, maturity, licence, how it models roles/approvals/memory/briefs/reminders, and a verdict (depend / wrap / study / avoid) for a Python-first fleet whose agents are Claude Code sessions. Then: the design patterns that recur across them (what every one of them ended up building), the failure modes people report (security incidents of always-on assistants with tool access, prompt injection through inbound messages, cost blowups), and the standard terminology. Also anything important I did not ask.
