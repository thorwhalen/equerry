# Ventures team: starter sources

Each entry: `[n]` title, URL, what it is for, jurisdiction, primary or secondary, last checked. The numbers are those of the REFERENCES list in this framework's research report `docs/research/domain-experts-for-a-solo-technical-founder.md`, so a number means the same source in every team's file. "Primary" means the statute, the issuer of a document or the originator of a method; "secondary" means a law firm's note, a guide or a vendor, not checked against the primary text. Re-verify quarterly.

Some primary pages refused automated fetches on the last check (EOS, NVCA, Carta, Justia); their content rests on search-indexed text and the secondary sources named alongside. Open them by hand before calling a claim from them certain.

## Equity and its clocks

- [13] Carta, A founder's guide to the 409A valuation. <https://carta.com/learn/startups/equity-management/409a-valuation/>. For: a 409A before grants; validity up to 12 months or until a material event such as a SAFE or priced round; safe harbour through an independent appraisal. `[US]`. Secondary (vendor). Last checked 2026-11-09.
- [14] Gibson Dunn, Focus on QSBS provisions in the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (2025). <https://www.gibsondunn.com/focus-on-qsbs-provisions-in-the-one-big-beautiful-bill-act/>. For: the QSBS clock for stock issued after 4 July 2025 (50% at three years, 75% at four, 100% at five; $15 million cap; $75 million gross-asset test). `[US]`. Secondary (law firm). Last checked 2026-11-09.

- [31] Ervin Cohen & Jessup, Delaware SB 154, a shift in the corporate opportunity doctrine. <https://www.ecjlaw.com/ecj-blog/delaware-sb-154-a-shift-in-the-corporate-opportunity-doctrine-by-jeffrey-r-glassman>.
