# People team: checklists

Four procedures the people lead runs and the `checker` verifies against. Each section ends with "Done when", the line the checker tests. Numbers in brackets are entries of `sources.md`. People are referred to by their record id, never by name, in anything that leaves the instance.

## Classification test

Per person who works with a venture or the principal without being an employee. On engagement, then every six months, and whenever the way they work changes. One note per country of work.

**`[US]` common-law test** [26]. Write one evidence line for each factor, from the agreement and from how the work actually happens:

1. Behavioural control: who decides how, when and where the work is done; training given; detailed instructions.
2. Financial control: the person's own investment and tools; unreimbursed expenses; opportunity for profit or loss; services offered to the market; paid by the project or by the hour.
3. Relationship: the written contract's terms; benefits; expected permanence; whether the work is a key activity of the business.

State tests can be stricter than the federal one: which state's test applies is **to verify** with an employment lawyer.

**`[FR]` subordination link** [27]. The contract's label does not decide; the facts do:

1. Orders and directives: does the venture or the principal give instructions on how to do the work?
2. Monitoring: are hours, presence or execution controlled?
3. Sanctions: can the venture or the principal sanction a failure?
4. Integration: does the person work inside an organised service, on conditions set unilaterally, with no clientele of their own? (An indicator courts use; its weight is **to verify** with an avocat.)

Requalification applies from the first day of the relationship [27].

Output: per person and country, `risk: low | medium | high`, the evidence lines, and the action (change how the work is organised, change the agreement, or escalate to an employment lawyer on `high`).

Done when: every factor has an evidence line drawn from a document or an observed practice (not a guess), and every `high` has an escalation.

## IP assignment

Per agreement with anyone who creates something a venture or the principal will use: contractor, co-founder, advisor, employee, client staff contributing to a joint deliverable.

1. An agreement exists, is signed, and is dated before the work started; if not, it contains an assignment of work already done.
2. It assigns code, documentation, designs, data, inventions and content created under it, to the right entity (the one that will own and ship the asset, per the venture page).
3. `[US]` the assignment respects state limits and, in California, gives the required notice of the carve-out for inventions made on one's own time and equipment and unrelated to the business [28].
4. `[FR]` contractor and pre-incorporation work is assigned in writing; employee software vests automatically in the employer [29]. The form and the moral-rights questions are **to verify** with an avocat.
5. Background IP: what the person brings and keeps is listed in a schedule, and the licence the venture gets to it is stated.
6. Confidentiality, return of materials and data at the end, and what survives termination.
7. Contributions to open-source projects the venture runs: under the project's DCO or CLA (the engineering team's check).
8. The signed copy is where the venture page's sources say; the chain-of-title table (legal) points to it.

Done when: every item above is answered from the signed text, with the clause location, and every "no" has an action.

## Equity grant

Per grant of shares, options, BSPCE or advisor equity. Cross-domain: the event opens items for legal, finance and ventures too.

1. Board approval (or written consent) obtained or scheduled; the plan or instrument named.
2. Terms written: amount, instrument, price, vesting schedule, cliff, acceleration, what happens on departure.
3. `[US]` options: a 409A valuation current at the grant date (within 12 months and no material event since, a SAFE or a priced round counting as one) [13].
4. `[US]` restricted stock: the 83(b) election mailed within 30 days of the transfer, with proof of mailing kept; no late election is possible; Form 15620 is optional [11]. Put the deadline on the calendar the same day.
5. `[FR]` BSPCE: the company meets the conditions (under 15 years old, among others) and the recipient is eligible; the gains' tax treatment as reformed for 2025 is explained to the recipient by a professional, not the team [22].
6. Cap table updated (the ventures team's position table); QSBS or BSPCE clock dates noted.
7. The recipient's tax questions go to their own adviser; the team never answers them.

Done when: steps 1 to 6 each cite a document or a dated calendar item, and step 4 or 5 is either done or marked not applicable with the reason.

## Onboarding and offboarding

**Onboarding**, per person, before the first piece of work:

1. Classification note (above) for each country of work.
2. Agreement with IP assignment and confidentiality (above), signed.
3. Tax form collected for finance: `[US]` W-9 for a US person, W-8 otherwise; `[FR]` the contractor's registration and invoicing details, handled by finance.
4. Access granted least-privilege; every account, repository, drive and channel listed in the person's onboarding record, with the date.
5. A record exists for the person in the principal's people records (acquaint), with how they prefer to be reached (`acquaint reach`) and their writing style, so messages to them are written for them.
6. A first-week pack: what the work is, who decides what, where questions go.

**Offboarding**, per person, on the last day:

1. Every access on the onboarding list revoked or transferred; shared credentials rotated by the principal.
2. Equipment returned; work product delivered and in the venture's repositories.
3. IP and data return confirmed in writing; nothing of the venture's left on personal accounts.
4. Final invoice or payslip handed to finance; tax forms for the year noted.
5. Equity: vesting stops; repurchase or exercise windows noted for ventures and legal.
6. The person's record updated by a proposed observation (role ended, date), recorded by the calling session.

Done when: the onboarding access list and the offboarding revocation list match line for line, and every step has a date.
